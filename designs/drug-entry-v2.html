Overview content — with callout variants

Metformin is a biguanide antidiabetic agent and is the first-line pharmacological treatment for type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) in most international and NZ guidelines. It exerts its primary effect by reducing hepatic glucose output, principally via inhibition of complex I of the mitochondrial electron transport chain, which reduces ATP production and activates AMP-kinase.

💡 Mechanism: Activates AMPK → suppresses hepatic gluconeogenesis. Does not stimulate insulin secretion — no hypoglycaemia risk as monotherapy.

Beyond glycaemic control, metformin has established cardiovascular benefits in overweight patients with T2DM (UKPDS). It is also used off-label in polycystic ovarian syndrome (PCOS) to improve insulin sensitivity and restore ovulatory cycles.

⚠️ Withhold before contrast media: Hold metformin 48h before iodinated contrast in patients with eGFR <45 mL/min/1.73m². Restart once renal function confirmed stable.
🚨 Lactic acidosis risk: Rare but life-threatening. Risk increases significantly with eGFR <30, hepatic impairment, or acute illness. Do not use in eGFR <30.
Metformin does not cause weight gain and may be associated with modest weight loss, making it preferred in overweight patients. Unlike sulfonylureas, it does not stimulate insulin secretion.

Contraindications — Variant A: Colour-coded boxes

✕ Absolute contraindications
  • eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m² (risk of lactic acidosis)
  • Acute or decompensated heart failure
  • Hepatic impairment (ALT/AST >3× ULN)
  • Active or recent lactic acidosis
  • Acute illness with dehydration or haemodynamic instability
  • Perioperative period with significant fasting (withhold 24–48h)
! Cautions / Relative contraindications
  • eGFR 30–60 — use with caution; reduce dose if eGFR 30–45
  • Iodinated contrast media — withhold 48h if eGFR <45
  • Excess alcohol use (increases lactic acidosis risk)
  • Elderly patients — monitor renal function more frequently
  • Pregnancy — generally considered safe (discuss risk/benefit)

Counselling — Variant A: Numbered teal circles

  • 1 Take with food to reduce nausea and stomach upset — the most common side effects when starting.
  • 2 Start with a low dose and increase slowly over several weeks — this greatly reduces GI side effects.
  • 3 Metformin does not cause hypoglycaemia when taken alone, but may do so if combined with insulin or sulfonylureas.
  • 4 If you need a CT scan with dye (contrast), tell the radiologist you take metformin — it may need to be stopped temporarily.
  • 5 Avoid heavy alcohol use while taking metformin, as it increases the risk of a rare but serious side effect.

NZ notes — Variant A: Teal callout card

NZ notes

Funding: Metformin is fully subsidised on the PHARMAC community schedule. No Special Authority required. Available as standard-release and modified-release (Glucophage XR) tablets.

Scheduling: Prescription medicine (Rx). Pharmacist-only supply not available.

Formulations in NZ: 500 mg and 850 mg tablets (standard-release); 500 mg, 750 mg, 1000 mg tablets (modified-release). Modified-release often better tolerated for GI side effects.

NZ practice note: BPAC NZ recommends metformin as first-line in T2DM unless contraindicated. eGFR should be checked before initiating and regularly thereafter. PHARMAC funds the standard-release formulation at full subsidy; modified-release may have patient co-payment.

Fully subsidised Prescription only No Special Authority
