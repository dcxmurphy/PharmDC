Acne vulgaris
Dermatology Very common Inflammatory Chronic
Overview Pathophysiology Clinical features Management Drug summary Monitoring Counselling NZ notes
Overview

Acne vulgaris is a chronic inflammatory condition of the pilosebaceous unit, characterised by comedones, papules, pustules, nodules, and cysts. It affects up to 85% of adolescents and may persist into adulthood. It is the most common skin condition managed in community pharmacy settings in NZ.

The condition has significant psychosocial impact disproportionate to its physical severity — anxiety, depression, and reduced quality of life are common, particularly in moderate-to-severe cases.

Pathophysiology

Four interacting factors drive acne: (1) increased sebum production under androgen stimulation, (2) follicular hyperkeratinisation leading to comedone formation, (3) proliferation of Cutibacterium acnes (formerly P. acnes) in the obstructed follicle, and (4) an inflammatory immune response to bacterial antigens and sebum components.

Androgens, particularly dihydrotestosterone (DHT), upregulate sebaceous gland activity. This explains the typical onset at puberty and the role of hormonal therapies in female patients.

Severity grading
Mild
  • Comedones (open/closed)
  • Few papules or pustules
  • No nodules or cysts
  • Minimal inflammation
Moderate
  • Many papules and pustules
  • May extend to trunk
  • Some nodules (< 5)
  • Noticeable inflammation
Severe
  • Numerous nodules/cysts
  • Widespread inflammation
  • Scarring likely
  • Often needs specialist input
Non-pharmacological

Gentle, non-comedogenic cleanser twice daily. Avoid scrubbing, picking, or squeezing. Use non-comedogenic moisturiser and sunscreen. Dietary links (high-GI foods, dairy) are emerging but evidence is not yet conclusive — advise balanced diet rather than restrictive elimination.

Stepwise pharmacological management
1
Mild
Topical retinoid monotherapy
Adapalene 0.1% gel or cream once nightly. First-line for predominantly comedonal acne. Start 2–3 nights per week to build tolerance; increase to nightly. Allow 8–12 weeks for full effect.
In NZ, adapalene 0.1% is available OTC (Differin). Tretinoin requires a prescription.
2
Mild – moderate
Add topical benzoyl peroxide (BPO)
BPO 2.5–5% gel or wash applied morning (retinoid at night). BPO reduces C. acnes via oxidative killing and prevents antibiotic resistance when combined with topical antibiotics. Start low concentration to assess tolerability.
BPO bleaches fabrics — advise white pillowcases and towels.
3
Moderate
Add topical antibiotic (always with BPO)
Clindamycin 1% lotion or gel (morning) + BPO (separate application). Never use topical antibiotics without BPO — combination prevents emergence of resistant C. acnes. Limit to 12 weeks continuous use.
4
Moderate – severe
Oral antibiotic + topical regimen
Doxycycline 100 mg daily (with food) for up to 3 months. Continue topical retinoid + BPO throughout. Review at 3 months — if adequate response, stop oral antibiotic and continue topicals. Avoid in pregnancy. Tetracyclines not under 12 years.
Consider hormonal therapy (COCP) in females with cyclical flares — refer to prescriber.
5
Severe / nodular
Oral isotretinoin — dermatologist referral required
Isotretinoin is highly effective for severe or treatment-resistant acne but requires specialist initiation in NZ. Absolute contraindication in pregnancy (teratogenic) — mandatory contraception programme. Monitor LFTs, lipids, and mental health. PHARMAC-funded under Special Authority.

NZ-specific information

Adapalene 0.1%: Available OTC in NZ (Differin gel/cream). Not funded — retail price approximately $25–35.

Benzoyl peroxide: OTC, General Sale. Multiple brands available. Not funded.

Clindamycin topical: Prescription only (Rx). Funded on Community Schedule with no Special Authority for acne.

Doxycycline: Prescription only. Funded on Community Schedule. Also funded for rosacea and other indications.

Isotretinoin (Oratane): Prescription only — must be initiated by a specialist (dermatologist) in NZ. Funded under Special Authority (SA criteria: severe acne not responding to adequate courses of conventional therapy). BPAC NZ recommends GPs refer early rather than waiting for complete failure of all topical options.

BPAC NZ guidance: Prefer adapalene + BPO as first-line. Reserve oral antibiotics for moderate-severe. Co-prescribe topical retinoid with any oral antibiotic course to maintain remission after stopping antibiotics.
