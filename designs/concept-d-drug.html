Metformin

Mechanism of action

Metformin activates AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) in hepatocytes, primarily suppressing hepatic glucose output (gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis). Unlike sulfonylureas, it does not stimulate insulin secretion and therefore does not cause hypoglycaemia as monotherapy.

It also improves peripheral insulin sensitivity in muscle and adipose tissue, and modestly reduces intestinal glucose absorption. The net effect is a reduction in fasting plasma glucose and HbA1c without weight gain.

Clinical role

First-line pharmacotherapy for type 2 diabetes mellitus in most guidelines including BPAC NZ. Cardiovascular outcome data (UKPDS) supports reduction in macrovascular events in overweight patients. Also used off-label (and funded in NZ) for polycystic ovarian syndrome (PCOS) where it improves insulin resistance and may restore ovulation.

Standard dosing
Indication Starting dose Titration Maintenance Maximum
Type 2 diabetes 500 mg once or twice daily with meals ↑ by 500 mg weekly as tolerated 1000–2000 mg/day in divided doses 3000 mg/day

Dosing notes

Always take with or immediately after food — this significantly reduces GI side effects. Slow titration (weekly increments) improves tolerability. Modified-release (MR) formulations once daily at evening meal may further reduce GI symptoms if IR not tolerated.

If HbA1c target not met at maximum tolerated dose, add a second agent (SGLT-2 inhibitor or DPP-4 inhibitor preferred in NZ based on BPAC guidance).

NZ-specific information

PHARMAC funding: Metformin IR and MR are fully funded on the Community Pharmaceutical Schedule with no Special Authority required for T2DM. PCOS use is funded under Special Authority (SA criteria apply).

Available formulations: Metformin IR 500 mg, 850 mg tablets; Metformin MR 500 mg tablets (once-daily dosing). Brands: Mylan, Apotex, and others.

NZ scheduling: Prescription Medicine (Part I). Cannot be supplied without a prescription.

BPAC NZ guidance: Preferred first-line agent for T2DM. Continue in combination therapy. Review HbA1c at 3 months post-initiation; if target not met, add SGLT-2i (empagliflozin/dapagliflozin) or DPP-4i per BPAC algorithm.
